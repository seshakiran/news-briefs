## Today's top three stories

*Why these matter, with the receipts*

**Nvidia just handed out AI models you can run on your own machine — and the timing says everything about where the industry is heading.** The company released Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open 120-billion-parameter model built for "always-on agents," plus two lighter Bolt models sized to run locally on consumer-grade AI hardware. Super VL and the 74B Bolt are available now; the 4GB conversational model arrives October 23. The pitch is control: open weights, open datasets and training recipes mean a company can fine-tune the model on its own data and run it on its own infrastructure. Nvidia says Super VL is 5x faster than the next-best open agentic model — a vendor benchmark claim, not independently verified. What makes this more than a chip release is the direction of travel: the frontier labs keep chasing scale, while Nvidia keeps selling the escape hatch. https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nemotron-3-5-super-vl/

**Someone asked Meta's AI assistant to share a private chat. Then they found thousands of other people's shared chats with a single search query.** Security firm Claroc enumerated more than 6,800 share links on Meta's Muse assistant, because Muse builds each share's web address from the conversation's own title — and DNS records capture the whole address, title and unguessable ID included, the moment anyone opens the link. "It took one query," the researchers wrote. The handful of pages they opened contained material plainly not meant to be public: a candidate's notes preparing for a confidential executive-search call with a frontier AI lab, a CEO-only revenue review, and a loan application with the amount requested. Claroc reported the issue to Meta's bug bounty program on October 8 and reports no evidence anyone has misused the shares; Meta had not responded to Ars Technica at press time. Fair caveat: this is vendor research, self-reported and unaudited, from a company that sells security tooling for exactly this problem. https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/meta-muse-exposes-users-personal-and-business-secrets-when-they-share-conversations/

**Amazon isn't building a better chip — it's trying to unlock the door Nvidia closed.** AWS released TorchNeuron, which lets AI apps written in PyTorch (the free, open-source toolkit most AI software is built with) run on Amazon's Trainium chips by changing one line of configuration, no rewrite required. Amazon says about 85% of AI apps are written with PyTorch, and because Nvidia's CUDA software has become woven into that toolkit so thoroughly, its hardware is the default almost everywhere. So Amazon is contributing code upstream to PyTorch to strip out chip-specific assumptions. The honest question is whether hardware-agnostic tooling delivers real choice, or just swaps one headache for another. https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/amazon-is-taking-its-biggest-shot-at-nvidia-yet-with-new-torchneuron-dev-tool/
